Paper trading — simulated bankrolls, zero real risk

Every strategy candidate from the regime tournament trades this same live data in its own simulated portfolio, starting from the same capital. None of this touches the real exchange, even on testnet — it is a parallel, forward-only comparison used to build confidence before any strategy is trusted with real execution. Numbers here are never real money.

Go-live readiness scorecard

A computed answer to "are we ready to trust this with a live decision" - every threshold here is configured in config.yaml's readiness section, not invented on the spot. This never changes any live behavior by itself; it's a read-only report.

Tournament stability detail

Every symbol/regime combination, not just the currently-winning one - this is the raw evidence the "Tournament stability" criterion above is computed from.

Feature drift

Population Stability Index (PSI) comparing each symbol's live feature distribution against the training baseline of its currently-promoted model - an early warning that live market conditions may have moved beyond what the model was actually trained on, checked every cycle rather than only at the next scheduled retrain.

Execution quality

Real slippage (fill price vs. the price the decision was made at) and order latency, per symbol - measured from the exchange's own order responses, not assumed. Positive slippage means the fill was worse than intended, either direction.

Trade audit trail

The immutable, append-only record of every realized trade - entry and exit price, strategy, model confidence, market regime, and the reason for each side, together in one row. Records are never edited or deleted; a correction (if one is ever needed) appears as a new row referencing the one it supersedes, so the full history stays intact.

Backtest-vs-live correlation

Every symbol/candidate pair with enough live paper trades to say something - the raw evidence behind the "Backtest-vs-live correlation" criterion above. A candidate whose backtest Sharpe and live Sharpe move together is evidence the walk-forward methodology transfers to real conditions; if a candidate consistently backtests well but trades poorly live (or vice versa), that pair is exactly what to investigate first.
